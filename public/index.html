How are trends, signals and levels calculated?

Buy / sell trends use the Supertrend method. A trailing line follows the price at a distance of a few "average true ranges" (a measure of normal daily movement). While price stays above the line, the trend is a buy trend and the line ratchets up underneath it. A close below the line flips the trend to a sell trend, and the line jumps above the price. Each flip is marked with a Buy or Sell arrow. "Fast" reacts sooner but gives more false signals; "Slow" ignores more noise but reacts later. Trend strength comes from ADX: below 20 means the market is choppy and signals are less reliable. Trendlines (dashed) connect the rising lows of an uptrend or the falling highs of a downtrend. The signal list shows how each past signal turned out, so you can judge how well the method fits this market.

The app finds swing highs and lows: bars whose high or low is the most extreme within a window of surrounding bars. It then groups turning points that sit within about one average true range (ATR) of each other. Each group becomes a level. A level gets stronger the more times price has touched it, the more recently it was touched, and if it has worked as both a floor and a ceiling ("role reversal"). Levels above the current price are resistance; levels below are support. Thicker lines mean stronger levels. These are mechanical estimates, not predictions.
